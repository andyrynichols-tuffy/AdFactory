# @sully.finds: how the TikTok Shop machine works (study, 2026-10-27)

Data: every post from Sep 23 to Oct 26, 2026 (764 read; 695 posted in the last 31 days, each classified by
eye from its cover; all 533 videos downloaded, cuts counted, speech transcribed on this PC). Account:
14.9K followers, 1.6M likes, 7,170 posts. Views are TikTok's public counts on Oct 27. Sales per video
aren't public: Ad Factory's competitor tracker (Analytics → 🕵️ Competitors) estimates them from products'
public sold counts from Oct 28 on (upper bounds: a product's count includes everyone selling it).

## The short version

He doesn't win as an influencer. He runs a cheap, faceless assembly line:
**a few products in many colors × several near-identical one-take videos per product, every day, plus
flat-lay picture carousels that bring the reach.** Quantity of *tries* per product, not quality per post.

## 1. Output and rhythm

- ~22 posts a day Sunday–Friday, Saturday light (36 posts across 4 Saturdays).
- Posts land 3–8 pm (63% between 15:00 and 20:00), roughly every 10–15 minutes.
- Batch days: some days are nearly all carousels (Oct 4: 18, Oct 12: 16, Oct 20: 16), others are long video runs.

## 2. Product lineup: narrow, deep, colorways

- 3 product lines = 68% of posts: Pacsun Dylan baggy jeans (165), Blake extreme baggy jeans (167), Johnny
  fleece straight sweatpants (141). Each colorway is its own TikTok Shop listing, and each gets its own posts.
- Side products show up a few times each (Nike V5, camo cropped tee, H&M, Old Navy, Haus, energy drinks, gadgets).
- Haus (fitted tees/tanks/shorts) is a paid brand deal: 40 of 41 Haus try-ons carry TikTok's "ad" label.

## 3. Formats: what he makes vs. what gets seen

\*mostly one no-product skit ("Straight vs. Gay Face", 1.7M views): viral non-product posts feed the following.

- 13 of his top 20 posts are carousels. Carousel median views ≈ 4× the video median (3.5K vs 860).
- Carousels: 5–7 photos (sometimes 2) of the item laid flat on a wood floor, one big line of on-image text:
  "Fall essential: black baggy jeans", "Winter pick up: baggy jeans", "POV: you found the perfect pair…",
  a quote ("I need some affordable baggy jeans for this winter szn", "Bro PacSun is finally dumping their
  baggy jeans on TikTok" over a classroom photo). Original sound or a trending track.
- Worn videos get the fewest views yet stay ~a quarter of output: possibly they convert (to check with sales data).

## 4. The videos: one take, a script template, almost no editing

- Median 20 s, his voice over ~94% of it (4.5 words/s), median 1–2 cuts (125 videos have none). His face is
  almost never in frame; covers carry a TikTok-style product label ("PacSun Baggy Jeans").
- The same few scripts, re-read for every product/colorway:
  - **Sale news** (237 videos): "[Brand] is finally dumping their [item] on their TikTok shop… super nice wash,
    pairs with any outfit… on sale with free shipping."
  - **Two-person skit** (93): "Wait, I love your jeans / I just got them from PacSun / Why would you spend $70? /
    No, I got them on their TikTok shop."
  - **Seasonal**: "Just in time for this [fall/winter] season…"; **favorite**: "These are easily my new favorite…";
    **"I think I just found the perfect [shoe]…"** (few, but 3.7K median).
- Captions don't hook: they're sizing + hashtags ("6'1 160 wearing size 32x32🤝 #pacsun #jeans #baggyjeans";
  "He's…" in some, a girlfriend/other-person POV). The hook is the on-screen text and the first spoken line.
- Price/urgency is the angle: "finally dumping", "on sale", "$25 PacSun baggy jeans", "under $30", Black Friday.

## 5. Repetition is the strategy

- 55% of posts belong to a same-day group of 3+ with the same product, setup and script.
- Inside a group, the best take gets ~3× the group's median (one group in ten: 24×+). He's buying lottery
  tickets: TikTok picks which near-identical take to push.
- Groups come from shooting one setup at a time: 6–15 takes in a row (product swap, re-read the script).

## What this means for us (planner defaults)

1. **Lineup:** 2–4 product lines with many colorways and a live discount; give each colorway its own posts.
2. **Daily output:** 20 posts per channel on weekdays, ~5 on Saturday, posted 3–8 pm every ~15 min.
3. **Format mix per channel-day:** 25% flat-lay carousels (floor), 30% flat-lay voiceover videos (bed/couch),
   20% worn faceless, 10% held item, 10% skit/POV, 5% other or a brand deal.
4. **Shoot in sets:** one setup per block, 3–4 takes per product, swap product, same script template.
5. **Two channels, same products:** his POV ("I'm 6'1…") and your POV ("He's 6'1…", "I got my husband…"),
   always separate takes (no shared footage: TikTok flags reuse).
6. **Measure sales, not views:** the competitor tracker and our own Analytics decide what to keep.
